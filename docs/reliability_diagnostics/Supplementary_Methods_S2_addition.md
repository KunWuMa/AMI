# Additional post-hoc analyses

These analyses used the existing cohorts, D0-D1 feature window, start-of-D2 landmark and D2-D7 outcome window. All primary models, training-derived preprocessing parameters and public calibration transformations remained frozen. No primary model was redeveloped, no local outcomes were used for feature selection or refitting, and no new bootstrap estimates were generated. Tables S19-S23 are independent additions; Tables 1-3 and S1-S18 remain unchanged.

## Cohort selection and outcome observability (S19-S20)

The local selection comparison included 280 unique primary-AMI index patients who met the existing diagnosis, linked prior-ACS history and strict D2 residence criteria. Of these, 116 met the early-normal-Hb eligibility criteria and 164 did not. Eligibility groups were compared using observed values only. Exclusion reasons were classified hierarchically as uninterpretable early Hb, no interpretable early Hb, or early Hb below the sex-specific threshold. Hb differences induced by the eligibility definition were not interpreted as independent evidence of selection bias.

Among the eligible patients, those with and without interpretable future Hb were compared separately in the local (100 vs 16) and public (1,846 vs 88) cohorts. Frozen conditional-model probabilities were described in both groups; anemia outcomes remained unknown in patients without future Hb, and no conditional-model discrimination or Brier score was calculated for them. The frozen O3 probability of future Hb observation was also described. The full public-cohort comparisons include development, calibration and test patients and are descriptive, not held-out performance assessments.

Continuous variables are summarized by available-case median and interquartile range; binary variables by positive counts and observed-value denominators. Group totals and missing counts are provided separately. Continuous standardized mean differences (SMDs) use the difference in means divided by the square root of the mean of the two sample variances; binary SMDs use the square root of the mean Bernoulli variances. SMDs are signed as the first group minus the second. Equal constant values yield zero; unequal constants or insufficient available observations yield undefined SMDs. No significance threshold or causal interpretation was applied.

## Care composition and observation distributions (S21)

Admission-wide CABG coding defined the public CABG groups. Comparisons used the full outcome-observed cohort (379 CABG-coded and 1,467 without CABG coding) and, separately, the held-out test cohort (80 and 290). Frozen M2/M3 mean predicted risk and Brier score were summarized. Results from the full cohort are descriptive because it contains model-development patients. CABG-group AUROC was not estimated owing to the near-universal recorded-anemia outcome. The local CABG-name evidence was not used to classify the other local patients as CABG-negative.

Public test and local outcome-observed patients were compared for eight laboratory availability indicators, eight two-calendar-day indicators, the distributions of eight observation-day counts, ten pre-imputation missingness indicators and the physiological features. Missingness indicators were constructed before the frozen median imputation. Laboratory availability denotes at least one interpretable early value within the existing feature-availability boundary, not merely an ordered test.

## Probability reference and calibration precision (S22)

Within each outcome-observed evaluation cohort, the recorded event fraction p_ref defined a post-hoc descriptive constant-probability reference. Its Brier score was p_ref(1-p_ref), and Brier skill score was 1 - Brier_model/Brier_ref. This evaluation-sample reference is not a prospectively deployable external model. Expected events were the sum of frozen probabilities; E/O was this sum divided by recorded event counts. Here O_events denotes events, distinct from the observation indicator O.

Calibration intercept (intercept-only logistic calibration with the prediction logit as an offset) and calibration slope (logistic regression on the prediction logit with a free intercept) were reused from existing diagnostics. Available 95% intervals from 1,000 patient-level bootstrap resamples were reused without rerunning the bootstrap. Local M3-minus-M2 paired AUROC and Brier intervals used the existing paired resamples. Intervals not available in the frozen result tables are explicitly unreported, including M4 calibration intervals and public calibration intervals. The original resampling seed was 20261022; calibration-diagnostic resampling used 20261023. Local quintile calibration groups contain approximately 20 patients and cannot support precise risk-band conclusions.

## Frozen M3 decomposition (S23)

For feature j, z_j=(imputed x_j - training mean_j)/training scale_j and the raw logistic predictor is eta=b+sum(beta_j z_j). All medians, means, scales, coefficients and intercepts were read from the frozen model. Clinical/physiological, observation-day and missingness contributions were summed separately. With the frozen public calibration intercept a and slope s, final log odds equal a+s*logit(clip(expit(eta), 1e-6, 1-1e-6)). Calibrated contributions were s*beta_j*z_j, with the combined intercept a+s*b and an explicit clipping correction s*[logit(clip(expit(eta)))-eta]. The reconstructed probabilities were checked against the frozen predictions for every evaluation patient. Feature-group contributions are model-output decompositions, not causal effects or unique measures of information content; correlated features and coding choices influence them. Pattern summaries report predicted-risk distributions only, including the three local patients without albumin, without small-subgroup performance claims.

## Opportunity for future Hb observation (S20, supporting panel)

Calendar length of stay was the discharge calendar date minus D0. Remaining calendar days after D2 were this quantity minus two, bounded below at zero; these are date differences, not exact elapsed 24-hour periods. Potential inpatient calendar dates in D2-D7 included the discharge date and were capped at six; public dates were additionally truncated at recorded death. Local opportunity estimates use discharge dates and cannot reconstruct unavailable death or specimen timestamps. Public future Hb dates use specimen dates, whereas local dates use report dates. First future Hb was grouped as D2, D3 or D4-D7. Interpretable result counts reflect retained records under the existing preprocessing rules, not independent phlebotomies. Independently derived future-day and result counts were checked against the frozen cohort cache. These descriptive measures do not establish why a test was ordered or absent.
